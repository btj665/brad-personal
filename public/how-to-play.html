How to play

Sixteen casino games, six slot cabinets and thirty-five solitaires, all played against honest, published odds with play money. Here's how each one works — pick a game from the list, or read straight through.

Getting started

Make an account, set up your authenticator, and sit down.

The Tables is play money only — nothing here costs or pays real money. On the published site you sign in so your balance follows you from game to game and device to device:

  1. Sign up with a username, an email and a password.
  2. Set up an authenticator. Scan the QR code with an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy, 1Password, etc.) and enter the 6-digit code. You'll enter a fresh code each time you log in — it keeps your account yours.
  3. Log in with either your username or your email, your password, and the current code from the app.

You start with a bankroll of play chips. Pick any game from the top navigation; your balance is shared across all of them.

On your phone? Every table is built to work at phone width — the layouts reflow, and the bet controls sit within thumb's reach.

The shared wallet

One balance, every game, topped up when you're dry.

  • Shared balance. Your chips are a single pool. Win at blackjack and you've more to bring to the poker table — it's all one wallet, saved to your account.
  • Buying in. At the seated tables (poker, blackjack) you bring a buy-in to the felt and rack whatever's left back onto your wallet when you leave. Everywhere else your bets come straight off the balance.
  • Auto top-up. Go broke and the house stakes you again back to the base amount after a short cooldown, so you're never stuck with nothing.

Table basics

The handful of ideas every game here shares.

  • Chips. Click a chip to set your bet size, then place it on the spot you want.
  • Honest odds. Every shuffle and roll is random, and every payout is the real mathematical one — the house edges are the genuine casino figures, not tilted against you beyond them.
  • “To” vs “for”. A payout of 3 to 2 means you win 3 and keep your 2 stake (you end up with 5). 3 for 2 would mean your stake is part of the 3. The tables below use “to” throughout.
  • House edge is the average cut the house keeps over the long run. Lower is better for you; it's listed where it helps you choose a bet.

Card tables

Blackjack

Beat the dealer to 21 without going over.

The goal

Get a hand total closer to 21 than the dealer without busting (going over 21). Number cards count their value, face cards count 10, and an ace is 1 or 11 — whichever helps. A two-card 21 (ace + ten-value) is a blackjack, paid 3 to 2.

Your turn

  • Hit — take another card.
  • Stand — keep your total and pass to the dealer.
  • Double down — double your bet for exactly one more card.
  • Split — a pair becomes two hands, each with its own bet.
  • Surrender (when offered) — fold for half your bet back.
  • Insurance — a side bet against a dealer ace showing; rarely worth it.

The dealer then plays a fixed way: hitting until reaching 17. Beat their total and you're paid even money; tie and it's a push (bet back).

Rules you can set. This blackjack supports the full spread of real variants — dealer hits or stands on soft 17, surrender, re-splits, Spanish 21 and Free Bet style rules, and more. Each rule nudges the house edge; the game shows you where you stand.
Basic strategy (hit/stand/double per dealer up-card) cuts the edge to well under 1%. When in doubt: stand on hard 17+, always split aces and eights, never split tens.

Baccarat

Bet on Player or Banker — the cards play themselves.

You don't play a hand; you bet which of two hands — Player or Banker — will total closest to 9. Tens and faces count zero, aces one, and only the last digit of the total counts (so 7 + 8 = 15 → 5). A fixed rule decides whether a third card is drawn; nothing is left to choice.

Simplest edge: Banker is the best bet. Skip the Tie.

Pai Gow Poker

Make two hands from seven cards and beat the dealer on both.

You're dealt seven cards and split them into a five-card “high” hand and a two-card “low” hand. The only rule: the high hand must outrank the low. Both of your hands must beat both of the dealer's to win; win one and lose one and it's a push. A joker fills a straight, a flush, or acts as an ace.

  • Win both → paid even money (minus 5% commission).
  • Split (win one, lose one) → push.
  • Lose both, or tie a hand that “goes to the dealer” → lose.
The “house way” is a sensible default split the game can set for you — keep the highest five-card hand you can while still putting your best possible pair up front.

Ultimate Texas Hold'em

Heads-up Hold'em against the dealer — bet bigger, earlier.

Post equal Ante and Blind bets (and an optional Trips bonus). You and the dealer each get two cards and share five community cards. The earlier you commit your Play bet, the more you may wager:

  • Pre-flop: check, or bet Play at 3× or 4× the ante.
  • After the flop: check, or bet Play at 2×.
  • After the river: bet Play at 1×, or fold.

The dealer needs a pair or better to “open”. If they don't, the Ante pushes. Otherwise the best five-card hand wins; the Blind pays a bonus ladder on a straight or better, and Trips pays on trips-or-better regardless of the dealer.

Rule of thumb: raise 4× pre-flop with any pair, any ace, or two cards ten or higher. Only fold the river with truly nothing.

Three Card Poker

Three cards, one decision: play or fold.

Place an Ante (and optional Pair Plus). You get three cards; decide to Play (match your ante) or fold. The dealer needs Queen-high or better to qualify. Hand ranks run a little differently with three cards: a straight beats a flush.

When to play: raise with Queen-Six-Four or better; fold everything worse.

Caribbean Stud

Five cards against a dealer showing one.

Ante up; you and the dealer get five cards, one of the dealer's face up. Decide to raise (double your ante) or fold. The dealer qualifies with Ace-King or better:

  • Dealer doesn't qualify → ante pays 1 to 1, raise pushes.
  • Dealer qualifies and you win → ante pays 1 to 1, raise pays a bonus by hand rank (up to 100 to 1 for a royal).
  • Dealer qualifies and wins → you lose both.
Simple rule: raise with any pair or better (and a few ace-king hands); otherwise fold.

Mississippi Stud

Build a hand over three streets, raising 1×–3× each time.

Post an Ante. You get two cards, then three community cards are revealed one at a time. Before each reveal you either fold or raise 1×, 2× or 3× your ante. At the end your five-card hand is paid on a ladder — but a pair of sixes or better is needed just to get your money back:

Let It Ride

Three equal bets — pull two back if you don't like your cards.

Place three equal bets. You get three cards and share two community cards. Twice you get to decide: let it ride (keep that bet in) or pull it back. You're paid on your final five-card hand — a pair of tens or better:

Casino War

Highest card wins. The simplest game on the floor.

You and the dealer each get one card; higher card wins even money. On a tie you choose: surrender (lose half) or go to war — match your bet, burn three cards, and get one more each. Win the war and you're paid on the raised portion; a second tie is a bonus.

Going to war is the lower-edge choice over surrendering, but it's a coin-flip game either way — pure, fast fun.

Dice & wheels

Craps

Bet on two dice — the loudest, deepest table in the house.

A round starts with a come-out roll. The core bet is the Pass Line:

  • Come-out 7 or 11 → Pass wins. 2, 3 or 12 → Pass loses.
  • Any other number becomes the point. Now Pass wins if that point rolls again before a 7, and loses on the 7.

Once a point is set you can back your Pass bet with Odds — a bet paid at true odds with zero house edge. Other common bets:

BetWhat it does
Don't PassThe opposite side of Pass — bets with the house against the shooter.
Come / Don't ComeLike Pass, but started on any roll after the point.
Place betsBet a specific number (4,5,6,8,9,10) to roll before a 7.
FieldA one-roll bet on 2,3,4,9,10,11,12.
Best value: Pass (or Don't Pass) backed with maximum Odds is the lowest-edge bet in the casino. Skip the flashy one-roll center bets.

Spin the wheel; bet a number, a color, or a whole region.

Place chips on the layout before the spin. Bets pay by how unlikely they are:

Sic Bo

Three dice, a whole grid of ways to bet the total.

Three dice are rolled; bet on what they'll show. Small (total 4–10) and Big (11–17) pay even money and lose on any triple. You can also bet a specific total, a specific triple (huge payout, long odds), a pair, or a single number.

Lowest edge: Small and Big. The exotic totals and triples are where the house makes its money.

Big Six (Money Wheel)

Bet which segment the wheel stops on.

The wheel is divided into segments marked by multiplier (1, 2, 5, 10, 20) and a couple of jackpot symbols. Bet a symbol; it pays its number to one if the wheel stops there. The commoner the symbol, the smaller the payout.

Best of the segments is the 1-to-1; the big multipliers carry the biggest house edge. It's a simple, streaky wheel — bet small.

Machines

Video Poker

Draw poker against a paytable — skill genuinely matters.

You're dealt five cards. Hold the ones you want to keep and draw new cards for the rest; your final hand is paid on the machine's table. Unlike slots, your choices move the odds a lot — with correct play these are among the best games in the house.

Variants here

  • Jacks or Better 9/6 — the classic; a pair of jacks pays. “9/6” is the full-house/flush pay (the good one).
  • Jacks or Better 8/5 — a tighter paytable; same strategy, lower return.
  • Bonus Poker — extra for four-of-a-kinds.
  • Double Bonus 9/7/5 — bigger quad bonuses, slightly different strategy.
  • Deuces Wild (full pay) — all four 2s are wild; the whole paytable and strategy shift.

Playing well

  • Play up to ten hands at once — your held cards carry into every hand, each drawing independently.
  • Use auto-hold: the machine can highlight the mathematically best cards to keep. A great way to learn correct play.
First rule of Jacks or Better: never break a paying pair to chase a flush or straight unless you're one card from a royal.

Six cabinets, six different mechanics.

Set your bet and spin; matching symbols on a payline (or across the reels) pay out. Each cabinet plays differently:

The symbols

  • Wild — substitutes for other symbols to complete a win (sometimes with a multiplier).
  • Scatter — pays or triggers a bonus wherever it lands, not just on a line.
  • Bonus — collects toward a feature round.
Honest machines: each cabinet's return-to-player is a fixed, published figure — no streak is “due”, and the odds never change based on how you've been doing.

Keno

Pick up to ten of eighty; match the draw.

Choose up to ten numbers from 1–80. Twenty numbers are then drawn, and you're paid by how many of yours came up — the more you pick and the more you hit, the bigger the pay. Hit all ten and it's a jackpot.

It's a long-odds game by nature — the fun is in the near-misses and the occasional big hit. The payouts shown are the exact odds for each pick size.

Patience

Solitaire

Thirty-five patience games across the classic families.

A quieter corner of the floor: single-player patience, no betting. The aim is almost always to build the four foundations up in suit from ace to king. The families differ in how the tableau builds and how the stock is dealt:

Families
